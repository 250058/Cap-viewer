# Icon brief: CAP 뷰어
Assumptions: the house style of the icon-designer skills (dark grey squircle, off-white glyph, one accent) replaces the current blue-gradient ".cap" lettering; the accent is the gold data point the app already uses as its signature.
Product: opens PASCO Capstone (.cap) lab files on a phone — pages, graphs, tables, PDF export  ·  Audience: high-school and university physics students and teachers  ·  Category neighbors: line-chart-on-grid and bar-chart icons (analytics apps), document-with-folded-corner (file viewers), lab flask/atom/beaker (science apps), the Capstone and SPARKvue app icons (must not imitate)

## Concepts

### 1. Keystone  (axis: vertical)
Depicts: an arch's keystone
Metaphor: a capstone is the stone that locks an arch — the file format's own name, and the one piece that holds the lab together
Gestalt device: similarity-break — repeated pale voussoirs, one gold keystone at the crown
16px risk: the seams between voussoirs close up; the arch silhouette and the gold keystone must carry it alone
Distinct from: charts, documents and flasks — no data-app icon is an arch
Not a UI glyph: checked against home, magnet and eject; the arch has a flat base and a solid crown stone, which none of them have

### 2. Dynamics cart  (axis: vertical)
Depicts: a lab cart
Metaphor: the low-friction cart students push down the ramp in nearly every Capstone motion lab
Gestalt device: figure-ground — wheels and the plunger slot cut out of one solid body
16px risk: wheel knockouts merge into the body; the cart may read as a generic box
Distinct from: charts and flasks; it is the object, not the data
Not a UI glyph: checked against shopping cart (basket + handle) and truck/delivery; a flat slab on two wheels is neither, but the risk is real

### 3. Lab notebook  (axis: vertical, one controlled break)
Depicts: an open notebook
Metaphor: the file opens like a lab notebook, and its page carries the measured curve
Gestalt device: figure-ground — the rising position-time curve is cut out of the two pages
16px risk: the knocked-out curve is thin and disappears first; the open book survives
Distinct from: document icons (it is a bound open book, not a sheet) and chart icons (no axes, no bars)
Not a UI glyph: checked against book/reader and docs icons; the cut curve is the difference, and it is the part most at risk

## Palette
Background #2A2A2E · Glyph #E8E8EA · Accent #F2B632 (gold data point, already the app's signature colour)

## Revision (owner feedback)
The owner rejected name-based marks: the icon must show **what the app does** — open a lab file on a phone and read its pages, graphs and tables, then save a PDF. Concepts 1-2 are kept for the record; new concepts below. Every one carries the same moment: a measured position-time curve ending in the gold data point.

### 4. Phone with a graph  (axis: vertical, one controlled break: the curve)
Depicts: a phone showing a graph
Metaphor: the whole product in one object — your lab data, on your phone
Gestalt device: figure-ground — the curve is cut out of a solid phone body
16px risk: the curve knockout thins out; the tall phone shape and gold point must carry it
Distinct from: plain chart icons (no grid, no bars) and plain phone icons (the curve)
Not a UI glyph: checked against "mobile device" and stock/finance apps; the gold end point and the parabola (not a zig-zag) separate it from a stock ticker

### 5. Lab report page  (axis: vertical, one controlled break: the curve)
Depicts: a lab report page
Metaphor: the app turns a .cap file into readable pages — and saves them as a PDF report
Gestalt device: figure-ground — text lines and the curve cut out of one solid sheet
16px risk: text lines merge; the sheet and gold point remain
Distinct from: generic document icons by the graph that fills the sheet; no folded corner
Not a UI glyph: checked against "file"/"document" and "notes"; the curve and gold point are the difference

### 3. Lab notebook (revised, r1)
Kept as the third option: an open notebook whose pages carry the curve — workbook pages plus the graph.

## Recommendation
4. Phone with a graph — the only concept that shows both halves of the product (phone + lab data) in a single solid silhouette.
